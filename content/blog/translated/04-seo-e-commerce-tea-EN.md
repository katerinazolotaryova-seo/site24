## Body

### About the client

- Project — thetea.ua
- Industry — e-commerce
- Service — SEO promotion
- Target region — Ukraine
- Goal — fix the drop in organic traffic and support the migration of the site from the old platform to a new one
- Period of work — from December 2021, ongoing. At the time of writing, 18 full months of SEO work had been completed (there were pauses, which we describe below)

The online store thetea.ua operates in the specific niche of online sales of Chinese tea, where the key success factors are a wide assortment, product quality, and convenience of service. The tea market in Ukraine is highly competitive, so attracting customers requires unique tea varieties, detailed descriptions of flavours and beneficial properties, and convenient delivery and payment options.

Thetea.ua focuses on creating an atmosphere of tea culture, giving customers not just a product but a complete experience of choosing and enjoying a variety of exclusive teas.

#### Industry specifics

Niche product e-commerce projects on the Ukrainian market face strong competition both from comparably-sized stores and from giants such as Rozetka — and this has to be taken into account in promotion.

Tea sellers also face seasonal sales fluctuations, the difficulty of visualising different types of tea as a product (try showing in 2–3 photos how varieties of black tea differ and making those photos visually appealing), and the need to build trust in the store brand to turn one-time customers into regulars.

From an SEO perspective, this requires thorough work on site structure, technical optimisation, filter functionality, high-quality content, and building backlinks from strong external sites.

### Key results of our work

- Organic traffic grew 4× in 18 months of work*: from 11,928 users in December 2021 to 45,843 users in January 2025.
  *Work on the project had a pause due to internal business processes on the client side.
- According to Seranking, as of March 2025 we had moved 1,967 keywords into Google's top 10 for relevant and business-priority query groups.

- We resolved post-migration issues that restored organic search as a traffic channel and improved positions in results.

### Stages of the SEO work

In broad terms, the work on the project can be split into two distinct stages.

**The client's first and primary goal**, when they came to us, was to stop the drop in the site's organic traffic. At the time, TheTea was running on the Khoroshop platform, which has many advantages for creating and promoting online stores.

Organic search was an important sales channel for the store, and the traffic drop directly affected its profit. The client came to us on a recommendation, as experts who could fix the situation quickly.

**The second stage** began after the pause the client took to migrate the site to a new in-house platform. By the time they came back, traffic had started falling sharply due to mistakes made during the migration. Our task again was to influence the situation and stop the decline as quickly as possible.

#### Starting conditions

As of December 2021, the site was receiving 11,928 users per month from organic search.

As we noted, the site had been losing traffic for some time. December was down 6% compared with November 2021.

Khoroshop as a platform has a good level of technical optimisation, but the sites themselves can still contain content-level issues — for example, broken links or unoptimised meta tags. This project had the following gaps:

- Unoptimised meta tags. Titles were far too long and contained a lot of unnecessary duplicate information that only diluted page relevance — for example, "Jasmine tea — buy in an online store of elite tea in Kyiv, buy real Chinese tea with delivery across Ukraine in thetea.ua store."
- Descriptions were too dry, contained no special characters, and the resulting snippets were not particularly clickable — which limited site visits.
- Filter pages had not been optimised, which is critically important for online stores: filter pages can reach the top quickly and bring in well-targeted traffic, because users searching for more specific queries (for example, "pu-erh Yunnan province") have a more developed search intent and are more ready to buy.
- The site had a sufficient quantity of external backlinks, but the quality of the link profile was weaker than that of competitors.

### Proposed strategy

Over the course of working with the client, we proposed several types of strategy for different stages.

In the first stage, we ran an SEO audit of the site taking into account the technical specifics of the Khoroshop platform, built a semantic core for both top-level categories and subcategories and filter pages, and on that basis produced specifications for meta-tag optimisation and content writing — together with link building to strengthen positions in search.

The second stage of SEO began after the migration to the new platform. It included fixing technical issues, configuring internal linking and redirects correctly, refreshing page content, and acquiring backlinks.

In the longer term, we proposed a strategy of holding and lifting the site's positions in search — including monthly semantic-core refinement, content optimisation and filling, blog creation, high-quality non-aggressive link building, and quick response to changes in search algorithms or shifts in the store's assortment.

### Project implementation

#### SEO optimisation of the store on Khoroshop

At this stage we worked with the project for 4 months and completed the following:

- Built a semantic core for 100+ priority site categories. The work on this project was challenging and fascinating at the same time. For example, did you know what "elder's brows" are? Neither did we — it's a subtype of white tea.
- Optimised title and description meta tags, as well as page headings.
- Wrote specifications for category-page texts, and then produced, reviewed, and published those texts.
- Beyond category pages, we gradually started working on filter pages — building a semantic core for them, optimising meta tags, headings, and text, and opening filter pages with traffic potential for indexing.
- Ran a technical SEO audit of the site. It is worth noting that on the Khoroshop platform there is no access to the source code, since it is a cloud service — we can only fix content-level issues (for example, links with a 404 server response, unnecessary redirects, and so on).
- Acquired backlinks. After analysing the external link profile, we concluded that the site lagged behind competitors in external link quality — so we focused on placing links on more expensive and higher-quality platforms. We targeted sites in adjacent niches with a Ukraine geo and DR of 35+, but also manually checked organic traffic volume (we were interested in sites with 5,000+ monthly visits), traffic stability, and its distribution across pages.

After these activities, the traffic drop stopped and positive changes began. Already in spring 2022, monthly organic traffic growth was +18%.

After that, the SEO project went on pause, as the client was working on a new site on their own platform.

#### SEO work after the migration — stopping the organic traffic dip

The client came back to us roughly 2 months after the move to the new platform (June 2023), when the site started losing positions and organic traffic quickly. Our task was to stop the decline and continue further SEO promotion to drive traffic growth and consolidate TheTea's leading position in Google results.

The first conceptual issue with the migration was that many changes were implemented on the site at the same time. Specifically, the following were changed:

- design,
- CMS,
- page URLs,
- internal links from the main menu had been removed.

Changes like these always cause a dip in positions and traffic, but with a properly executed migration the losses can usually be minimised.

##### Head of SEO commentary

_The main problem with promoting the site at this stage was banal — lost time._

_We always advise clients to consult with us first before introducing major changes. Ideally, the new site should be rolled out on a test domain first, all tests should be run, all bugs fixed, and only then the update launched. But there were hard deadlines here, so the site moved "as is," and we ended up patching holes on the fly. As a result, Google managed to index the site with errors, which hit SEO performance._

##### Resolving the site's technical issues

Our specialists got involved quickly and produced a detailed specification for fixing the technical issues that had emerged during the migration, with a focus on the most critical points needed to recover lost positions rapidly.

In particular, we wrote a separate specification for fixing the 301 redirects, which had the following issues:

- Redirects from Russian-language pages of the old site were configured to point to the Ukrainian-language version of the new site. The core problem here is that with the redirects set up this way, Russian-language anchors of external links pass anchor weight to the Ukrainian page — which is not relevant — while the Russian-language pages do not receive any of that weight at all. Redirects should be configured per language version: Russian-language pages of the old site should redirect to Russian-language pages of the new site.
- Redirects were going through 2–3 hops. All redirects fired via a mask first to an equivalent URL without a trailing "/" and only then to the fully new URL. This is bad because each 301 redirect loses part of the page's internal weight.
- Some redirects were configured to 404 pages.
- Incorrectly configured redirects pointed to the home page or to a category, subcategory, or main blog page — even when a relevant page existed on the new site.

As a result, we prepared a redirect map from old URLs to new ones, describing correct redirect implementations — a necessary procedure when URLs change on a new site.

It should also be noted that some redirect problems can always be solved at the level of general redirect rules. For example, URLs "with /" can be redirected to URLs "without /" at the end. For others, you have to manually match "old-new" URLs. In that case, for a large site, mapping every URL is unrealistic, so we only process the most important ones, namely:

- those covered in the semantic-core work (category, subcategory, and filter pages);
- those that had traffic according to Google Analytics;
- those that had positions for keywords tracked in Seranking.

##### Checking Googlebot accessibility of site pages

Whenever a site is migrated, Googlebot accessibility of all the main elements must be verified. For example, in our case the links from the main menu on the old site were available for indexing by search engines. In the new version of the site, they only became visible to the user — the search bot could not see them. As a result, internal linking was disrupted, and internal anchor weight did not flow to target pages.

##### Matching content on the old and new site

A very simple error was uncovered, but one with a significant negative effect: text had not been carried over to 2 important pages — the home page and the Pu-erh tea page. Since those very pages brought in the most traffic, this mistake made the drop substantial — but, fortunately, easy to fix.

##### Filter functionality

For the new site, it was important to work on filter functionality. After the migration, all filter intersections were open for crawling and indexing by Googlebot, which meant purely technical pages got into the Google index. This is bad for SEO, since indexing and analysing such pages spends the site's "crawl budget" — meaning Googlebot may not reach the genuinely useful, optimised pages. A large volume of such "garbage" also harms the overall quality assessment of the site, and the site ends up with lower positions.

These were the priority tasks for the fix, with major impact on indexing and ranking. After the critical changes were implemented relatively quickly, we returned to a detailed full technical audit of the site, during which we additionally identified and jointly resolved:

- correct redirect configuration;
- closing technical pages from indexing;
- correct rendering of the top menu;
- page loading speed;
- correct implementation of multilingual setup;
- replacing relative URLs with absolute ones in the rel="canonical" tag;
- fixing breadcrumb issues, and so on.

##### Interim results

After the technical fixes, organic traffic stopped falling and started to grow, and the site recovered its positions quickly — visible on the Search Console screenshot.

#### Ongoing work with TheTea

After resolving the post-migration traffic drop, we moved to the stage of monthly SEO promotion.

Each month we expanded the semantic core and optimised individual pages. After working through all commercial pages (categories, subcategories, and filter pages), we moved on to building a semantic core and writing specifications for blog pages.

Every 3 months we ran technical audits and wrote specifications for the development team to fix the issues that appeared. Since the site runs on a custom in-house system, technical issues periodically emerge — that is a normal process, and they need to be spotted and addressed.

We acquired external backlinks from other sites, following the principle "fewer but higher quality" described above.

Expanding the blog and regularly adding articles also produced strong results. We tracked the latest trends and search queries and created detailed specifications for copywriters.

We also took a systematic approach to work that helped the client substantially increase organic traffic and convert site visits into purchases. To do this we:

- built city-targeted pages on the pattern "type of tea + city" — we wrote specifications for the implementation, proposed meta tags, and produced detailed text specifications. These pages performed well in Google's top results and brought additional traffic.
- wrote specifications for optimising pages about tea accessories — teapots, tea figurines, gaiwans, and so on. The same applies to pages about tea flavours and properties.
- implemented additional internal linking from product cards to category pages at various levels.
- wrote specifications for filter implementation so that, when a filter page is closed from indexing, the link to it is not generated on the parent page.
- exported a list of URLs of product pages that had traffic but for which the product had run out — such pages had been deleted and their URLs returned a 404 server response, which is not ideal for SEO. We set up 301 redirects to similar products.

This meticulous, systematic work month after month — by our team and the client's team — delivered solid results.

### Conclusions

This case is another reminder of how important it is to consult SEO specialists before introducing significant changes to a site — that is the best way to avoid a sharp drop in organic traffic. The faster the causes are analysed and critical technical errors corrected, the smaller the losses in traffic and rankings will be.

In addition, the TheTea case shows that a competent SEO approach during a site migration can avoid major position drops and even deliver growth — provided redirects are configured correctly, the site structure is preserved, and every change involved in moving to the new platform is taken into account.
