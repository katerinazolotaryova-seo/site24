## Body

### About the client

- Project — [thetea.ua](https://thetea.ua/)
- Industry — e-commerce
- Service — PPC advertising
- Target region — Ukraine
- Goal — scale and optimise advertising campaigns to increase orders and revenue from the PPC channel
- Period of work — from March 2024, ongoing

Thetea.ua is an online store specialising in the online sale of Chinese tea.

It operates in a narrow product segment where the key competitive advantages are a wide assortment, the availability of rare varieties, and consistent product quality.

The site is aimed at retail buyers in Ukraine and provides convenient mechanisms for product selection, checkout, delivery, and payment. The catalogue is structured by tea categories and related products.

Store content includes information about the origin of each tea, its flavour profile, and its properties, which helps the customer make an informed choice.

### Project specifics

In general, promoting an online store with a narrow specialisation — in this case Chinese tea and tea ceremony accessories — is always a challenge.

A food niche of this kind is marked by high competition in search results, elevated requirements for the quality of visual content, and product certification standards.

At the same time, the site's audience is clearly defined: it consists of true tea enthusiasts who understand varieties and look for an authentic product. The advertising strategy had to be focused on exactly this audience.

Successful campaign work in this niche requires deep segmentation, precise keyword selection, active use of negative keywords, regular optimisation, and a personalised promotion approach.

### Client goals

When the cooperation began, the client already had paid search campaigns set up and running stably. The main problem they came to us with was a lack of growth in performance: results stayed at the same level even during high-demand seasons.

The client also wanted targeted, individually configured promotion of specific product positions to drive interest in particular tea varieties that had high sales potential.

With that in mind, the main tasks were:

- scaling the advertising campaigns;
- increasing order volume and total revenue;
- personalised promotion of selected products.

### Key results of our work

- Transactions grew **2.3×** (from 940 to 2,143 per month)
- Revenue grew **2.5×**
- ROAS grew **by 200%** (from 333% to 523%)

### Stages of the PPC work

The cooperation started in March 2024, in the middle of a high-demand season for tea products. So the first stage was rapid intervention in the already running campaigns: we ran an audit, fixed key mistakes, and quickly optimised settings to make the most of the active sales period.

The next step was developing tailored promotion strategies for specific product positions with high potential — this let us shift the focus to products the client treated as priority and strategically important.

The third stage is already ongoing systematic optimisation: we regularly analyse campaign performance, adjust bids, update negative keywords, and test new audiences and formats to maintain steady sales and revenue growth.

### Starting conditions

When work began, the client already had active advertising campaigns that had been running for some time. The work started with a comprehensive audit of the Google Ads account to identify optimisation opportunities and fix shortcomings.

**Transactions — 940 per month**

**ROAS — 333%**

### Account audit

#### 1. Performance Max campaigns

The main campaign was "Performance Max | Sales | UA 2," which covered the entire product range. However, it had an unnecessary asset group with elite tea that was not functioning properly and had no logical rationale.

The audience settings included an excessive number of different signals (search terms, every available segment), which made segmentation and testing impossible. For proper performance, each audience segment should have been placed in a separate asset group.

In addition, the other Performance Max campaigns were effectively used as shopping campaigns: with no extra creatives, images, or text. There was no site-change tracking in place, so the system was showing outdated categories — including ones with no products in stock.

We also found campaign duplication — situations where campaigns had identical settings, promoted the same products, and ran simultaneously, which created internal competition between campaigns.

Overall, the account structure was chaotic: campaigns were created by different people at different times without unified logic, leading to internal conflicts, duplicates, and reduced efficiency.

#### 2. Search campaigns

The only campaign that consistently delivered high results in terms of revenue and ROAS was the brand campaign.

Two more campaigns — "Pu-erh" and "Priority Products" — generated revenue but had low ROAS. Technically they were set up well: a well-built semantic core, properly distributed keywords across groups, relevant ad copy, and negative keyword lists. Despite that, the economic efficiency remained insufficient.

### The proposed strategy

The main goal of our strategy was to bring order to the advertising account, optimise existing campaigns, and gradually expand the structure with a focus on category-based semantics and audience testing.

#### Performance Max campaigns

- The first step was to refresh the main all-products campaign with the Ukrainian feed: removing unnecessary asset groups, splitting audiences into separate groups to test the efficiency of different segments.

*A feed is an XML file containing structured information about all the products in the store: names, descriptions, prices, image links, availability, and so on. This file is uploaded to Google Merchant Center and used by advertising campaigns to display products in Google results. It is from this feed that the system knows what products are in the store and how to display them correctly in ads.*

- The next step was to create an analogous campaign with a Russian-language feed to cover a wider user audience.
- The following stage was to launch new Performance Max campaigns for each main site category (green tea, red tea, white tea, tableware, and so on), starting with Ukrainian-language versions.
- After collecting statistics, we planned to separate top-selling products into a dedicated campaign to reinforce focused promotion.

#### Search campaigns

- The brand campaign was kept as is, since it delivers stable results.
- For the "Pu-erh" and "Priority Products" campaigns, we planned point optimisations: excluding underperforming keywords that consumed budget without returns, and testing different automatic bidding strategies.

The key principle we followed was building a clear account structure overall — one that would allow us to better analyse the performance of each direction and quickly make changes to improve results.

### Project implementation

#### Performance Max optimisation

At the starting stage, we decided to focus on Performance Max campaigns, since one of them was already partially functional and could be quickly optimised to generate stable revenue.

We adjusted the existing campaign based on the Ukrainian-language feed:

- removed unnecessary asset groups,
- added ad copy and images,
- created new ad groups split by audience type: search queries, interests, users who had already made purchases, and all site visitors. This let us test the efficiency of each segment individually.

In parallel, we created a copy of this campaign adapted to a Russian-speaking audience — with corresponding texts and a Russian-language feed.

#### Building category-based Performance Max campaigns

The next stage was the gradual creation of separate PMax campaigns for each product category: Shu Pu-erh, Sheng Pu-erh, Pu-erh resin, Oolongs, and so on.

To avoid "traffic cannibalisation" between campaigns (the situation where several campaigns compete with each other to show the same product), we implemented feed splitting through rules in Google Merchant Center. By modifying product IDs (for example, adding a prefix such as "pm_" or "cat_"), each campaign received its own unique feed even when the source data was a single feed.

**Technical implementation of the feed markup included:**

- using attribute rules in Merchant Center to split feeds by ID;
- tagging products in the feed for later identification in campaigns;
- creating conditions under which Google treats those feeds as independent, allowing the same product to appear several times in results simultaneously — occupying more shopping positions on the results page.

#### Additional segmentation by price

A separate piece of work was splitting products by price segment. We created separate campaigns for low-, mid-, and high-priced products. This let us apply more precise bidding strategies:

- for low-priced products — the **Target ROAS** automated strategy,
- for high-priced products — **Maximize Conversions**.

This logic was also implemented through Merchant Center, using rules and feed filters.

That way we achieved maximum control over the campaigns, reduced internal account competition, and built a flexible foundation for further optimisation.

### Search campaigns and results over time

In the first stage of work, the search campaigns went through minimal but targeted changes.

- Keywords that spent budget but did not produce revenue were turned off.
- Ad copy was refreshed: existing wording was corrected and new variants were added.
- Automated bidding strategies were switched from **"Target ROAS"** to **"Maximize Conversions"** to activate sales under limited traffic conditions.

As a result, from March to August 2024 there was steady growth in the number of transactions. Revenue, which had grown already in the first month after the optimisation, stayed at a similar level afterwards — campaigns ran stably, without sharp dips or peaks.

Later on we continued scaling: launching additional PMax campaigns, experimenting with new settings, and refining already active campaigns based on the data we received.

### Interim results

In the first months of the strategy we managed to:

- create Performance Max campaigns covering both the full product range and the main tea categories;
- run PMax campaigns split by price segment;
- run baseline optimisation of search campaigns.

Thanks to that preparation, the advertising account was fully ready for the seasonal demand surge. By mid-August both order volume and revenue began to rise visibly. Part of this was seasonality — cold weather traditionally drives interest in tea — but the key factor was the structural work on the advertising campaigns.

In the period that followed we continued scaling: launching additional PMax campaigns, experimenting with new settings, and refining already active campaigns based on the data we received.

#### Expert commentary

Over the whole period of work we created more than 30 campaigns — mostly Performance Max, plus a standard Shopping campaign and a DSA (Dynamic Search Ad) campaign. The campaigns were varied: covering the entire assortment, single categories, individual products, and price segments. We worked with two language feeds (Ukrainian and Russian).

We did a lot of testing. What works well in a general campaign does not always deliver in a stand-alone one. Sometimes, on the contrary, an isolated campaign "fires" better. The approach was therefore flexible: ineffective decisions were turned off, successful ones were scaled.

Most recently we have focused on ROAS optimisation: we analysed the dynamics, paused weaker campaigns, and kept only those showing steady growth. That let us raise efficiency without increasing the budget.

In short, the project relied on the approach we apply in most e-commerce cases: structure, hypotheses, fast reaction, and analytics.

### Results achieved

The advertising strategy — built on phased optimisation of existing campaigns, the introduction of Performance Max with clear segmentation, and regular hypothesis testing — delivered a clear and measurable outcome.

Over the year of work:

- Transactions grew **2.3×** (from 940 to 2,143 per month)
- Revenue grew **2.5×**
- ROAS grew **by 200%** (from 333% to 523%)

### Next steps

To maintain positive momentum, this year we plan to focus on the following directions:

- **Continued optimisation** of already running campaigns with a focus on increasing monthly revenue and reducing acquisition cost. This includes regular performance analysis, work with keywords, budgets, and bids.
- **Launching new campaigns** for individual products, categories, or price segments — informed by the results of earlier experiments.
- **Launching Demand Gen campaigns**, which have recently come out of beta. This campaign type is aimed at attracting a new ("cold") audience and nudging it toward a purchase. For best efficiency, it is preferable to use video creatives or banners, and to engage look-a-like audiences based on customer lists or remarketing.
- **Active use of video format.** It is recommended to create a brand YouTube channel and run video ads for new or focus products. Video format is already showing strong potential in Facebook advertising — and it makes sense to scale that into the Google ecosystem too.

### Conclusions

The results were achieved thanks to systematic work on the advertising account. The key success factors were:

- weekly optimisation;
- competent feed work: building marked-up XML structures and segmenting by category and price;
- adaptive management of budget and bids with a focus on effective products and categories;
- flexibility in decision-making — quickly switching off weak campaigns and scaling successful ones;
- precise audience work and personalised promotion.

The The Tea project showed that even in a narrow, highly competitive niche, with the right account structure, campaign setup, and thoughtful analysis of results, you can deliver steady growth in performance without significantly increasing the budget.
