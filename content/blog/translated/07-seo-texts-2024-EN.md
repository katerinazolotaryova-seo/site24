### AI in copywriting: the paradox of growing demand

[Original H2 in UA: "Фокус на нульовій позиції" — replaced with a meaningful EN H2; see Notes above]

The fastest growth in Ukraine and worldwide was in queries related to the ChatGPT neural network. Their volume grew 30× over the course of 2023, drawing the attention of many companies, specialists, and media. As a result, one of the most popular topics in copywriting right now is the use of artificial intelligence. Clients often order articles such as:

1. Top 20 AI-based content generation tools.
2. How to learn to write prompts for ChatGPT: a detailed guide.
3. The best alternatives to ChatGPT and Midjourney.
4. Where to learn how to use artificial intelligence.
5. How to automate your work with AI and earn more.

But a rather interesting paradox emerges. The more often clients order articles connected with artificial intelligence, the more negatively they react to the use of AI in copywriting itself. Many clients insist that texts be checked for AI use. This is especially true for authored content — blog articles, social media posts, expert opinions, reviews, and so on. Some clients even write instructions for checking texts with 2–3 services, trying to catch the smallest trace of AI use. How productive and important that actually is is an open question with no clear answer. But copywriters should definitely prepare for additional text checks and possible client complaints.

Popular topics also include:

- SaaS services: how-tos, reviews, and guides.
- SEO: do-it-yourself site promotion, training, and trends.
- IT: education, career choice, and reviews of new technologies.
- iGaming: rankings of gaming sites, advice, and comparisons.
- Products and services: current realities of markets and trends.

### The end of the "word-mill" copywriter

[Original H2 in UA: "Фокус на нульовій позиції" (repeated) — replaced with a meaningful EN H2; see Notes above]

In 2024, an entire class of copywriters may disappear completely — the authors who earn by producing large volumes of text with minimal quality requirements. With a trained hand, they generate up to 30–60 thousand characters a day, earning a decent income even at minimum rates. Their typical tasks are writing technical text "fillers," articles for link building, and descriptions of identical products. But demand for those authors — sometimes ironically called "woodpeckers" because of how they work — is gradually disappearing.

There are several reasons for that:

1. Google keeps raising its requirements for texts. Low-quality material is now treated as spam and can hurt a site's ranking.
2. Simple, mechanical work is handled excellently by artificial intelligence. There are specialised services for generating outreach posts, rewriting, and other similar tasks.
3. The approach to site promotion is changing. Expert content based on real experience plays an ever-growing role in it.

For copywriters, quality is moving to the forefront. The real professionals stay on the market — those who have built their own style, have expertise in particular fields, and are willing to spend time on raising their qualifications. Authors who earn through sheer volume — or treat copywriting as just a side gig — will have to change or pick a different specialty.

### Readability, UX, and voice search

[Original H2 in UA: "Фокус на нульовій позиції" (repeated) — replaced with a meaningful EN H2; see Notes above]

The gap between copywriters and writers is gradually closing. The days when every keyword had to be inserted into a text in its exact form, ignoring any language norms, are passing. The main value becomes UX — user experience, that is, how well sites meet visitors' expectations. To get a high rank in search results, it is no longer enough to use every keyword on the list — the text has to be readable, well-structured, and easy to take in.

In addition to uniqueness, copywriters now need to check "nausea" (keyword overuse) and "wateriness" metrics. The first reflects how often particular words are repeated; the second — the share of text without semantic value. So texts have to be short, informative, beautifully written, simple, and clear — pitched at every segment of the target audience.

Another important trend is the growing number of voice queries. People are increasingly using virtual assistants on their devices, dictating queries and listening to the answers. To adapt to that, you need to use keywords in conversational form — the way the phrases are actually spoken in everyday dialogue. It also helps to add a list of common questions built from trends and search results.

### Proofs, and proofs again

While Google's E-E-A-T formula was introduced back in 2014, it remains highly relevant. And copywriters should pay particular attention to its last letter — Trustworthiness. Whenever you use a specific fact, claim, or statistic, you must include a link to the source. That improves an article's standing in the eyes of search bots.

One more tip — not only cite the source, but also mention it in the material itself. List the names of the researchers or the name of the company that ran the survey, and make it as easy as possible for readers to find that information themselves — that is a signal of well-thought-out UX.

### What will the copywriter's 2024 look like?

The main insight from WordFactory: everything will change, and it will no longer be the way it used to be. The market is shifting, and you have to adapt. Cynical as it may sound, the law of natural selection still applies: the stronger ones survive.

In 2024, requirements for copywriters may rise further due to changes in search algorithms and the growing role of artificial intelligence in content generation. Authors will have to use all of their creative potential to write readable, beautifully crafted texts with a clear structure. They will also have to provide relevant information and back facts and figures with links to sources.

Sounds complicated? But successful copywriters already rely on most of these requirements today. For beginner authors, however, it may indeed be hard. To get up to speed faster, they should start by studying the basic principles of SEO, getting familiar with artificial intelligence, and monitoring current trends.
