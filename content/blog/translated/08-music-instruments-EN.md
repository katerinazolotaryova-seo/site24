## Body

### SEO promotion of an online store of musical instruments

A case study about how, over 18 months of work, we:

- moved 3,705 (92%) keywords into Google's top 10 and 1,922 (48%) into the top 3;
- grew SEO traffic almost 24× — from 667 (March 2022) to 17,205 (November 2023) SEO visits per month.

### Project start

The work began with our joint campaign with Khoroshop, in which we offered small SEO packages for sites on that platform. After that start, the client wanted to continue cooperation in a full format.

**Client:** a specialised store selling guitars, other musical instruments, and accessories and components for them.

Because of its guitar specialisation, the site has fewer products and pages than the main competitors such as muzline.ua, jam.ua, and others. At the same time, the assortment of the Guitars section is substantial and does not lag behind competitors.

### Project goal

Reaching the top 3 in priority directions — Acoustic, Classical, and Electric guitars — while using a minimal budget for external links, since the start of the cooperation coincided with the beginning of the full-scale invasion.

### Project specifics

Thanks to the fact that the site runs on a well-optimised platform, technical on-page work was easy to deliver, and we were then able to focus on the semantic core, site structure, and content optimisation, as well as on growing external links.

### On-page optimisation

The Khoroshop platform on which the site is built works on a cloud model — the client cannot make any edits to the site's code, but can create pages, add content (text and meta tags), and build any number of categories, subcategories, and filter pages. The ability to easily create optimised filter pages is precisely what allows you to achieve strong results in SEO for online stores.

The advantage of this platform is that it has good technical optimisation — no internal duplicates are created, the site has good loading speed, and structured-data markup and required tags such as canonical and hreflang are generated automatically.

At the same time, simple technical issues related to content and structure can still appear on such a site, and they need to be identified and resolved.

For example, in this project we had to:

- update the URL structure to make URLs human-readable;
- fix internal and external 301 URLs to point to final targets so they returned a 200 server response straight away;
- replace images that were broken or returning a 301 redirect;
- fix URLs returning a 404 error;
- replace duplicate meta tags with unique ones (in this case they were not the result of technical duplicates but of identical names for different subcategories under different categories — for example "Cases and accessories" inside both acoustic and electric guitar categories);
- implement templates for meta-tag generation;
- add alt attributes for images;
- fill empty category pages with product cards or remove them;
- compress images that were too large.

### Building the semantic core and on-page optimisation

The key task of this work block was to expand the site structure as much as possible, build landing pages for each individual semantic cluster, and add optimised meta tags and texts that are both useful for people and include all the keywords needed to rank.

Work on the semantic core should be done section by section, starting with the priority ones. The order can be defined by the client based on sales volume or category profitability — or proposed by the SEO specialist taking into account the search volume and competitiveness of each product group.

The first stage builds the full semantic core for each product group, including every variety and subtype. A separate semantic cluster is created for each subtype. When there is doubt about whether a subtype is the same or different from a search-engine point of view, we analyse Google's top for the queries we are comparing. For example, if we are unsure whether "Classical guitar" and "Acoustic guitar" form one cluster or two, we must study Google's results for those two queries. If the top is mostly occupied by the same pages — that is grounds to point both clusters to a single page; if they are different — then to two separate pages.

Next, a dedicated landing page is assigned to each cluster. It can be either a new category or subcategory page, or a filter page. The latter is used extensively in e-commerce optimisation, since filter-page optimisation lets you create landing pages for a large number of clusters. For such a page to count as optimised, it must have:

- unique optimised meta tags and an H1 heading;
- a placed text with keywords;
- a human-readable URL;
- a link to itself inside the filter functionality on the parent category;
- a reference in sitemap.xml;
- a sufficient number of products (at least 5–6, but preferably benchmarked against the average product count on the equivalent competitor page).

Meta tags and content specifications are written following the general principles of SEO optimisation. The title includes keywords; the description is composed taking into account both keyword presence and conveying the advantages of the site or product. Texts are written for users on the basis of analysis of leading competitors in each niche.

One potential issue with on-page optimisation of an online store with many worked-through filter pages is that pages can end up looking quite similar in content — and then Google can determine relevance incorrectly and rank a different page.

For example, at one point in this project, the page "Electric guitars for beginners" was ranking for the query "Electric guitars" — because the assortment of both pages was similar and the text topic was close. The solution in this kind of situation can be:

- renaming some product cards that belong to filter results (although this is not always possible from a business standpoint);
- expanding the text on the broader category;
- shortening the text on the filter page and using fewer broad keywords;
- strengthening the category page with quality anchor links.

### Internal linking

For additional internal linking on this site we use a special block of links on product cards pointing to the corresponding filter pages. This passes additional weight to filter pages, which has a positive effect on the growth of the site's positions.

**Content writing.** Since the SEO budget — including for copywriting — was limited, texts were written first for top categories and the most frequent filter pages. For less frequent categories we built small FAQ blocks.

It also helped that the product pages contained mostly strong, unique texts. Overall we can say that projects with strong, lengthy product-page descriptions are promoted much better.

### Building the backlink profile

In any work with external links, several principles must be followed:

- links should come from high-quality platforms — pay attention to organic traffic volume, a low count of other outbound links, and a high DR value in Ahrefs (25 and above);
- you need to obtain links from sites in your topic or an adjacent topic, in your geolocation;
- maintain a stable, even volume of monthly link purchases — avoid long pauses and sharp spikes of large buys.

In general, when choosing donor sites you should follow the principle "fewer but better."

It is also important to note that buying external links should start after technical optimisation is finished and optimised content is placed on priority sections. That way you can judge which internal pages are worth getting links to — because if the site has history and on-page SEO has been done well, some pages can already reach the top 10 without external links. With a limited SEO budget, it is important to put links to the pages that would not otherwise reach Google's top.

In this case, since the project effectively started from zero, in the beginning we bought a 50/50 mix of anchor and non-anchor links. In the following months the proportion shifted toward roughly 70% anchor and 30% non-anchor links — but that is only acceptable when the site is also receiving non-anchor links naturally; otherwise there is a risk of pessimisation for over-optimisation.

When forming the link strategy we followed the principle of maximum variety in the target pages for external links — possible thanks to the wide assortment and the worked-through structure. For example, we bought links both to the general "Electric guitars" page and to subtype and brand pages within that category. As a result, the site's position growth moved across a broad set of queries at the same time.

### Conclusions

Results of this project over 18 full months of work (from March 2022 to December 2023 with short breaks):

- 1,922 keywords in Google's top 3 (48% of the total promoted query set);
- 3,705 keywords in Google's top 10 (92%);
- SEO traffic growth of 24× (from 667 visits in March 2023 to 17,205 in November 2023);
- leading site visibility among competitors with a relatively low SEO budget, evidenced by the small overall link mass.

We are highly satisfied with the project's results. A key role was played by the fact that the site is built on the well-optimised Khoroshop platform. From our extensive experience, we can say that projects on this platform show faster and stronger growth than sites on other CMSes.

Another big plus in working with this client was the fairly large assortment, which made it possible to compete with the long-established market leaders.

We continue working on the SEO promotion of this site, focusing on further growth in traffic and sales.
