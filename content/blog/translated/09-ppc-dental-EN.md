## Body

### PPC advertising for a dental clinic entering the US market

**Project period:** June 2023 — ongoing
**Analysis period:** 5 June — 27 November 2023
**Region of promotion:** Queens, New York
**Client:** a dental clinic offering a wide range of services, including preventive care and dental treatment, whitening, implant placement, root canal treatment, veneers, and many more.

#### Client advantages

- the clinic offers the full range of dental services;
- it is open on Sundays;
- no prior appointment is required for emergency care;
- the team values the client and looks for an individual approach to everyone;
- it offers the minimum necessary but maximally effective intervention (the goal is to preserve the natural tooth wherever possible);
- new clients get discounts on services.

### Starting point

When the client came to us, they had a working Google Ads account but with campaigns paused. Conversions were not tracked in analytics. On top of that, the quality of the existing campaign was low: all keywords had been added in broad match into a single campaign with a budget of 1 cent per day.

### Promotion goal

Acquire the maximum number of new clients.

### Analytics setup

To start, we asked the client to place the Google Tag Manager code on the site and configured tracking for the key events:

- form submission;
- click on the phone number from mobile (so we could understand how many people tried to get in touch by phone — there was no call tracking).

We also set up remarketing audiences. We created a separate audience for each service page so that remarketing campaigns could be launched for specific services.

#### Dental services promoted in the first stage

- Dental implants
- Porcelain veneers
- Invisalign aligners. The last one came with complications (more on this below).

### Campaign types

Google Ads search campaigns.

We chose this campaign type specifically so that ads would reach the most targeted audience: people searching for the services we were advertising.

The search campaigns were set up from scratch following the general methodology:

1. Using the Keyword Planner, we collected search queries with a frequency of 10+ impressions per month. This level of detail matters when setting up paid search in an expensive, competitive niche — it gives more room to capture clicks that are not the most expensive.
2. Unsuitable queries were used to build a negative keyword list to reduce impressions on irrelevant queries.
3. Suitable keywords were split into logical ad groups. We built a convenient structure so that results would be simpler and more effective to analyse and manage.
4. Keywords were used in phrase match (to avoid collecting irrelevant queries and to keep synonymous ones).
5. Ad copy highlighted the clinic's advantages. At this stage, prices were not included in the ads (per the client's preference).
6. We added every available extension:

- Structured snippets
- Sitelinks that used special characters to attract user attention and stand out from competitors. They were added at the campaign level (each service had its own sitelinks).
- Callouts
- Phone number
- Address
- Logo and business name

Extensions matter because:

- ads look more attractive against competitors;
- we can convey more useful information to a potential client;
- they take up more space in results (and competitors are left with less);
- the more assets there are, the higher the account optimisation score Google assigns.

Overall the ads passed moderation quickly, and despite the health-related topic, all of them were approved.

Throughout the month we ran **scheduled optimisation:**

1. Regular search query cleanup.
2. Creating additional negative keyword lists and expanding the existing list so ads were not shown for off-target queries.
3. Pausing low-performing keywords (those triggering less targeted impressions).
4. Expanding the semantic core based on Google's recommendations.
5. Expanding the semantic core based on actual search queries.
6. Ad copy work (testing headlines to improve quality and raise the account optimisation score).
7. Bid adjustments by age. We applied +15% bid adjustments for users in the 25–34, 35–44, and 45–54 age groups — so our ads would be more likely to show to this age range, since the statistics collected showed that people of this age are more interested in our services.
8. Working through Google recommendations to raise the account optimisation score.
9. As the work progressed, some ad assets started getting disapproved, so we filed appeals to reinstate those ad assets.

### Issues we dealt with

There was a situation where Google was showing our ads in the "Invisalign aligners" campaign alongside ads from the manufacturer of the aligners and their resellers. In other words, we were competing with the manufacturer of the aligners — Invisalign. In reality we were not selling the aligners themselves but advertising the service of installing them. Because of this, clicks were expensive and not always targeted. When we identified the situation, we quickly removed all the more general queries that could be interpreted in two ways. As a result, site traffic dropped significantly and the clicks did not get cheaper. The campaign turned out to be unprofitable, so we decided to pause it and concentrate the campaign budget on other services.

#### In the second stage, at the client's request, we added 6 additional directions:

- Emergency dental care
- Root canals
- Wisdom tooth extraction
- Tooth extraction
- Dentures
- Dental crowns

To reduce budget "dispersion" (since there were monthly spending limits), we merged some services — for example, Tooth extraction and Wisdom tooth extraction. The result was 8 search campaigns advertising 9 services. Later, Dental crowns and Porcelain veneers were turned off because they were the least effective — clicks were expensive and brought few clients — and the budget was reallocated in favour of Dental implants and Dentures (combined into a single campaign), where results were strong.

An interesting situation appeared with **geo targeting**. Initially we chose a radius of 7 and 4 miles from a point on the map. Then we decided to test the hypothesis that people would want a closer option and narrowed the targeting to 2 miles. In the end, however, we saw that in our case a 7-mile radius worked best. The client confirmed that people are perfectly willing to travel that distance to them. That said, for the Emergency dental care campaign, a 4-mile radius also proved effective.

During the second stage, on top of scheduled optimisation, we added an ad in the Emergency dental care campaign that included the phone number — so that someone in need of urgent help could call the clinic without going through the site.

#### Issues we dealt with

At this stage we added prices to the ad copy. That decision was driven by the need to deal with non-qualified phone calls. The client reported that people were starting to contact them looking for cheap implants ($150 for 4 implants) — at a time when the real price was significantly higher. After looking into it, we discovered that competitors had started running banner ads featuring a $150-for-4-implants price along with the client's clinic name. After people saw the banners, they searched for the clinic in Google and contacted us asking about that exact service — while we were paying for every click from the ads, and those clicks turned out to be low-quality. So we decided to write the prices directly in the ad copy, so that people looking for cheap services would not click and burn our budget. We advised the client to seek help from lawyers and properly file a complaint about the competitors, since under US law manipulating prices in advertising carries administrative liability.

According to the Auction Insights report, we held second place, with a 11.39% share of impressions received.

#### In the third stage, we decided to test a Performance Max campaign for advertising the Implants service.

The decision was made to test a hypothesis: since the service is expensive and serious, people spend a certain amount of time choosing a clinic to turn to. First, we wanted people to know they could come to us for this service. Second, if we kept reminding them of our existence over a period of time, that could persuade them to submit a request.

So we decided to build two targeting variants:

1. Topic-based reach (a relatively new feature in Performance Max). For this we set the most popular keywords as topics — so the ads would be shown to people interested in implants while they were browsing the internet.
2. Targeting people who had visited the page on the site with the description of the Implants service. The purpose was to remind those who had already considered us as a partner.

Currently the ads are running — even though with limitations (the reason given is "Personalised advertising: Health"). We have impressions and clicks in both asset groups, with a relatively low cost per click of $0.99 and 0.88% CTR.

### Advertising results after 6 months of cooperation

- 139,649 impressions
- 18,521 clicks
- 13.26% CTR
- 1,975 conversions
- 10.66% conversion rate
- $25.21 cost per conversion
- $49,797 ad spend over the reporting period

Comparing the last month against the first:

- cost per click was reduced from $6.64 to $2.54 in the final month;
- the number of contact requests grew from 5 to 636 per month;
- cost per conversion dropped from $574.12 to $23.58.

As a result of six months of cooperation, we delivered 1,900 contact requests for the client (1,300 form submissions and 700 phone-number clicks from mobile). That averages 11 requests per day.
