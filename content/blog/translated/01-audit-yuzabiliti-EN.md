## Body

### Website Usability Audit

Even with steady traffic, a site can fail to sell, fail to engage, and fail to do its business job. It is critical that every visitor can quickly find the product or service they need and complete an order from the moment they land on the site. Complex navigation, overloaded pages, unclear buttons, and extra steps in the order form all lower conversion and drive up bounce rates.

According to research from the Baymard Institute, on average up to "70% of potential conversions are lost not because of weak demand, but because of user experience problems."

A usability analysis helps you identify weak spots and interface gaps, improve behavioural signals, and simplify how users interact with your site.

The outcome of a UX audit is a set of practical recommendations for improving the site. Implementing them lets you **raise the conversion rate**, **reduce the bounce rate**, and ultimately increase overall site profitability.

As competition grows and traffic gets more expensive, UX has become one of the key drivers of site performance. That is why a usability audit is no longer an option for large projects only — it is a working tool for businesses of any size.

According to the Nielsen Norman Group, "users most often leave sites when they are forced to think about what to do next."

### What is a usability analysis?

**A UX audit** is a comprehensive service from the **Site24** agency that examines how convenient the site is for users. The usability audit shows how easy it is for visitors and returning customers to find the information they need and place an order online or get in touch with the company.

The audit looks not at design as such, but at the **interaction logic**: page structure, behavioural scenarios, clarity of controls, and decision-making speed. It usually combines expert evaluation (based on UX principles and heuristics) with data analysis — user behaviour, exit points, and sales funnels.

Google also takes behavioural metrics and UX signals into account in ranking, which has a positive effect on the growth of a site's positions and organic traffic.

### Why and when to run a usability audit

The main task of a usability audit is to turn the site not just into a digital business card, but into an **effective sales tool**. To do that, you need to identify and remove every barrier that stops visitors from completing the target action.

A usability audit makes sense in several situations:

- **When launching a new site.** It ensures that the design, structure, and navigation are clear and convenient for users.
- **Before launching a large-scale advertising campaign.** Very often advertising fails to deliver results because of problems with the site itself — an audit helps you avoid that situation.
- **When site performance indicators drop.** If you notice a drop in conversion, an increase in bounce rate, or other issues, an audit is worth running. It will identify the causes and possible solutions.
- **On a regular basis.** User expectations and habits change, new technologies and UX design trends appear. It is important to review the site periodically and update it accordingly.

**A UX audit is especially needed** when there is traffic but few sales. Lifting the conversion rate is the core goal of a UX audit.

### Website usability audit: the checklist

Here is how we run a usability audit and the key aspects our specialists examine:

- **Navigation.** Checking how easy it is to reach any page and to return to the home page.
- **Page structure.** Reviewing the logic of the structure, how it aligns with the tasks at hand, and the visibility of key elements (order form, CTA, contacts).
- **Design and visual presentation.** Font readability, visual accents, element contrast — everything that affects perception.
- **Interaction scenarios.** How simple and clear it is to fill in forms and complete other target actions.
- **Site responsiveness.** Whether elements display and work correctly on mobile devices.
- **Loading speed.** Pages and elements must load quickly and reliably.
- **Content readability.** Text structure, headings, readability, and other factors.

The result is not abstract advice but a **concrete checklist of problems and improvements** tied to the site's business goals.

### Types of sites that need a usability audit: who should run one, and why

A usability audit is useful for any type of site. There are, however, several categories where it is essentially **mandatory**, because user convenience on those sites has a direct impact on sales — and therefore on business profitability.

Here are those categories and why an audit matters for each:

- **Online stores.** The catalogue structure affects how quickly and conveniently users can find products. The convenience of the cart and checkout directly affects sales volume.
- **Corporate sites and service sites.** It is important that users quickly find the services and information they need, and that the ordering process is intuitive.
- **Landing pages.** Their job is to convert a visitor into a buyer or client. An audit helps surface even the smallest issues that get in the way.
- **Media and information portals.** They tend to have a complex structure and a large, growing volume of content.
- **Startup sites.** At an early stage, an audit helps avoid critical mistakes and audience loss.
- **SaaS projects and user dashboards.** Complex interfaces and non-obvious scenarios reduce engagement and retention.
- **Sites with a high share of mobile traffic.** Mobile UX is often the source of critical mistakes and losses.

It is important to run audits not only when problems appear. It is far more cost-effective to find and fix gaps before they create losses for the business. The **Site24** team is ready to help you do exactly that.

### Stages of a website usability audit

The usability analysis process breaks down into several stages. It is important for the client to know how the work proceeds and what they will receive as a result.

A professional approach always starts with setting goals: which business metrics need to improve (conversion, page depth, leads, sales).

- **Data collection and analytics.** We gather data on visits and user behaviour. We identify where users drop off and which pages cause difficulty.
- **Expert evaluation.** We assess structure, design, and content against a checklist. We manually identify problem spots that reduce order volume and increase traffic loss.
- **User scenario testing.** We check how convenient it is to complete target actions: find information, find a product or service, place an order.
- **Reporting and recommendations.** We prepare a detailed report and recommendations to eliminate gaps and grow conversion and profitability.
- **Detailed and structured action plan.** We sort the recommendations by priority and expected impact (priority items and items to be implemented during further site development).

So when you order a usability audit from us, you get a **strategic improvement plan** that delivers maximum impact at an optimal investment level.

### What to do after the audit

The real value of a usability audit shows itself only after implementation. Practice shows that the best results come when recommendations are built into the product or marketing process — rather than left as "a document for the sake of a document."

**Step one — focus on priorities.** According to the Baymard Institute approach, "fixing even a handful of critical UX errors can deliver a much larger effect."

Next comes the implementation plan:

- **quick wins**
- **mid-term improvements**
- **changes that require redesign or development**

After implementation, it is essential to re-measure metrics — conversion, session depth, behaviour in key scenarios.

It is important to understand that **UX is not a one-off task**. The best digital products use audits as a recurring optimisation tool, returning to the analysis after scaling, audience changes, or the launch of new traffic channels.
